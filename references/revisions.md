# Reviewing a revision

Read the prior decision letter, all reviewer reports, the response letter, the revised manuscript, and tracked or highlighted changes when supplied.

Create an issue ledger with one row per prior concern:

- original issue and requested outcome;
- author response;
- manuscript location of the claimed change;
- status: resolved, partially resolved, unresolved, or made moot;
- evidence and any new consequence.

Verify changes in the manuscript; do not treat the response letter as proof. Judge whether the underlying concern was resolved, not whether the authors followed the reviewer's preferred wording or method. Accept a well-justified alternative solution.

Focus the rereview on unresolved issues and problems introduced by revision. Do not move the goalposts by adding unrelated requests unless a new change creates a material issue or a serious problem was genuinely missed earlier; explain why the new concern is being raised.

Structure the report around overall progress, unresolved major issues, newly introduced material issues, minor residual points, and—if requested—a recommendation consistent with the remaining issues.
