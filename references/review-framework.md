# Review framework

Use the sections relevant to the manuscript type. Absence of a conventional IMRaD heading is not itself a defect; judge whether the necessary information is present and findable.

## Editorial fit and contribution

- Is the question within the venue's scope and meaningful to its audience?
- Is the contribution stated accurately and distinguished from prior work?
- Are novelty and importance claims supported by current primary literature rather than rhetoric?
- Does the title describe the design and content without overstating it?

## Abstract

- Does it accurately preview the objective, design, setting or data, key methods, principal results, uncertainty, and appropriately bounded conclusion?
- Are numerical claims consistent with the main text and tables?
- Are keywords appropriate for discovery?

## Introduction and conceptual basis

- Is the problem or knowledge gap specific and supported?
- Are objectives, hypotheses, or research questions explicit and aligned with the design?
- Does the literature review supply the conceptual or theoretical basis needed to understand the methods and interpretation?
- Is significance explained without exaggeration?

## Methods and research integrity

- Is the design correctly identified and appropriate to the question?
- Are setting, dates, participants or units, eligibility, sampling, interventions or exposures, outcomes, and procedures sufficiently described?
- Are primary versus secondary outcomes and planned versus exploratory analyses distinguishable?
- Is sample-size reasoning appropriate? Are controls, randomization, allocation concealment, blinding, matching, and clustering addressed where relevant?
- Are measures valid and reliable for the intended construct and population?
- Are exclusions, missing data, attrition, transformations, multiplicity, model assumptions, sensitivity analyses, and deviations handled transparently?
- Are effect sizes and uncertainty reported, not only thresholded significance tests?
- Is enough detail, code, data, or material availability information supplied for reproducibility, subject to ethical and legal limits?
- Are preregistration, protocol, trial registration, ethics approval, consent, animal welfare, funding, conflicts, and data governance reported where applicable?
- Are sex, gender, race, ethnicity, disability, geography, or other relevant population dimensions handled with justified definitions, inclusive reporting, and appropriate limits on generalization?

Do not equate reproducibility with literal replication in every discipline. Judge transparency against the methods, epistemology, and norms appropriate to the field.

## Results

- Do analyses correspond to the stated questions, design, outcomes, and analysis plan?
- Are participant or sample flow, exclusions, denominators, missingness, and adverse or null findings visible?
- Are estimates, uncertainty, and practical magnitude reported and interpreted correctly?
- Are subgroup, post hoc, or exploratory findings labeled and protected against overinterpretation?
- Are text, tables, figures, supplements, and underlying counts mutually consistent?
- Do figures and tables add understanding, use legible labels and units, avoid distorted axes or visual encodings, and state sample sizes?

## Discussion and conclusions

- Does the interpretation follow from the results and design, with association kept distinct from causation?
- Are alternative explanations, robustness, external validity or transferability, and meaningful limitations discussed?
- Is comparison with prior work balanced, including conflicting evidence?
- Are conclusions proportional to effect size, uncertainty, bias, and study scope?
- Are recommendations for practice, policy, or future research justified rather than generic?

## Article-type adaptations

### Systematic or scoping review

Assess protocol or registration, reproducible search strategies, database and grey-literature coverage, eligibility criteria, duplicate screening, extraction, risk of bias or critical appraisal, synthesis choice, heterogeneity, publication bias, certainty of evidence, excluded-study accounting, and PRISMA flow. Distinguish an incomplete report from a biased search or invalid synthesis.

### Narrative review or perspective

Assess purpose, selection logic, coverage, balance, conceptual synthesis, handling of contrary evidence, and whether claims exceed the non-systematic method. Do not demand systematic-review machinery unless the article claims systematic completeness.

### Qualitative research

Assess methodological congruence, researcher positioning and reflexivity, sampling rationale, context, consent, data generation, analytic process, credibility or trustworthiness, evidence supporting themes, disconfirming cases, and transferability. Do not impose quantitative validity tests.

### Computational, mathematical, or machine-learning work

Assess task definition, dataset provenance and leakage, train/validation/test separation, baselines, ablations, metric suitability, uncertainty or variability, hyperparameter and compute reporting, code/model/data availability, robustness, fairness and subgroup performance, threat model where relevant, theorem assumptions, and proof correctness. Separate benchmark gains from real-world utility.

### Theory, humanities, or conceptual scholarship

Assess the thesis, originality, argumentative validity, source selection, interpretive method, engagement with counterarguments, evidentiary accuracy, and contribution to the relevant conversation. Adapt reproducibility language to traceability of sources and reasoning.

## Severity and requested remedy

Use severity based on consequence, not annoyance:

- **Major:** could change confidence in a central claim, ethical acceptability, interpretability, reproducibility, or the main contribution. Explain whether it is plausibly repairable with existing data and scope.
- **Minor:** localized reporting, clarity, consistency, or presentation issue that does not materially alter the central assessment.
- **Optional:** worthwhile extension that is not needed to support the paper's present claims. Label it explicitly so it cannot be mistaken for a condition of publication.

Recommendation calibration:

- Use **minor revision** when the core work is sound and remaining changes are bounded.
- Use **major revision** when substantial analysis, reframing, reporting, or validation is required but the existing study can plausibly support a publishable paper.
- Use **reject** sparingly: for a decisive scope mismatch, non-repairable ethical or validity problem, unsupported central premise, or a remedy that amounts to conducting a substantially new study. A long list of comments alone is not a reason to reject.
- If the venue offers “reject and resubmit,” distinguish it from terminal rejection and state what foundational reconstruction would make a new submission reviewable.

A useful comment has four parts: **location → issue → consequence → proportionate remedy**. If a concern depends on an outside source, cite it and state the inference.

## Integrity concerns

Report concrete indicators privately to the editor: unexplained image duplication, impossible or inconsistent data, undeclared overlap, ethics or consent gaps, implausible chronology, citation manipulation, or text similarity. Use cautious language such as “I observed…” and “This may warrant editorial verification.” Do not accuse authors, contact institutions, run intrusive investigations, or expose confidential content.
